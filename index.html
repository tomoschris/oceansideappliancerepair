How to Diagnose Common Dryer Problems

Dryers can stop working for several reasons, and diagnosing the issue early can save time and money. Dryer Won’t Start
If your dryer doesn’t start, check the power supply first. Make sure it’s plugged in and the circuit breaker hasn’t tripped. If power isn’t the issue, the problem could be a faulty start switch, door switch, or thermal fuse. Use a multimeter to test these parts.

Dryer Runs But Produces No Heat
This is a common issue. For electric dryers, the heating element may have burned out. For gas dryers, check the igniter or gas valve coils. Also, inspect the thermal fuse and high-limit thermostat. Any of these parts can prevent the dryer from heating properly.

Dryer Makes Loud or Unusual Noises
Thumping, squealing, or grinding sounds usually point to worn-out drum rollers, a damaged belt, or a faulty idler pulley. Remove the dryer panel and spin the drum by hand. If you hear noise or feel resistance, replace the affected parts.

Clothes Take Too Long to Dry
If drying time is longer than usual, check the lint filter and vent hose. Blockages can slow airflow and cause overheating. Clean out the filter and vacuum the vent hose. Also check the blower wheel, which moves air through the dryer. If it’s loose or clogged, it may need replacing.

Dryer Shuts Off Mid-Cycle
This problem can be caused by overheating. The thermal fuse or thermostat may be tripping. Poor airflow is a common cause of overheating. Clean the vents and check internal sensors. If the problem continues, the motor might be failing and may need replacement.

Drum Not Spinning
If the motor runs but the drum doesn’t spin, the belt may be broken or slipped off. Open the cabinet and check the belt’s condition and position. If the belt is intact, inspect the motor and idler pulley for damage.

Burning Smell or Excess Heat
Unusual smells may mean lint is trapped near the heating element or motor. Unplug the dryer and clean inside the cabinet. If the smell continues, stop using the machine and call a technician to inspect it safely.

Diagnosing a dryer problem starts with a careful look at the symptoms. Some fixes are simple, but others need a trained technician.
